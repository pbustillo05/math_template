# Exemplary exposition and mathematical refereeing

Research date: 2026-11-15. Scope: craft transferable to publishable mathematical research, with lecture notes and surveys included as requested. This is a reading map, not a ranking of the greatest papers. Expository awards provide identifiable evidence of reputation; popularity and research prizes alone do not establish writing quality.

## How to read the evidence

- **Sample inspected** means the indicated passages of the primary mathematical text were read through the web tool; it is not a mathematical verification of the whole work.
- **Award-backed** identifies an award specifically for exposition. Where an author's lifetime award supports reputation, this is explicitly distinguished from an award for the selected article.
- **Selected model** means our recommendation, without a claim of consensus.
- All mathematical papers/books below are **remote-only, unacquired survey sources**. No original payload, DOI API metadata, alpha bibliography key, or cryptographic retention record has been added to `references/`. The acquisition table below records that open gap rather than implying a retained corpus.
- The paragraphs headed “Craft to borrow” are our observations/inferences. They are not experiments establishing that imitation improves AI writing.

## Fast shortlist for this repository

| Need | Start with | Transferable feature |
|---|---|---|
| A research introduction that earns its definitions | E02 Ghrist; E11 Kerodon | Concrete mathematical limitation, then the object that resolves it |
| A short proof with visible architecture | E07 Milnor | State the mechanism, isolate two useful lemmas, return to the main theorem |
| A demanding reader's first encounter with an abstraction | E04 Leinster; E12 Vakil | Operational examples, explicit conventions, motivation before machinery |
| A survey that places a theorem in context | E06 Granville; E13 Williamson | Scope, history, conceptual obstacle, and selective detail |
| Precise reusable reference material | E10 Stacks | Explicit data, conditions, caveats, and stable local cross-references |
| A constructive style report | R01 MAA; R03 SIAM | Summary, major issues, minor issues, exact locators, stated review limits |

## Award-backed articles and author-backed exemplars

### E01 — Ravi Vakil, *The Mathematics of Doodling* (2011)

- **Text:** [author-hosted PDF](https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/files/monthly116-129-vakil.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4169/amer.math.monthly.118.02.116).
- **Reputation:** 2014 Chauvenet Prize; official [MAA recipients list](https://maa.org/chauvenet-prizes/), 2014 entry, inspected.
- **Inspection:** published pp. 116–118, opening question, definition of a radius neighborhood, Theorem 1 and its two-inclusion proof.
- **Craft to borrow:** A visual question is made precise in stages; notation arrives when it does work. The proof names its two obligations before discharging them. This is a model for exposition that remains mathematical while giving the reader a reason to proceed.
- **Limit:** A broad expository article; its playful voice need not fit a specialist journal paper.

### E02 — Robert Ghrist, *Barcodes: The Persistent Topology of Data* (2008)

- **Text:** [author preprint](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~ghrist/preprints/barcodes.pdf) (403 on direct retrieval); [readable published-text mirror](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/ghrist1.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-07-01191-3) (resolver retrieval blocked).
- **Reputation:** 2013 Chauvenet Prize, official MAA list.
- **Inspection:** §§1–1.3 and selected §2 material, published pp. 61–66; original article text, not a secondary summary.
- **Craft to borrow:** Three principal themes orient the survey. Each construction answers an identifiable problem with the preceding representation. Definitions of Čech and Rips complexes follow that need; figures and subsequent algebra explain their roles.
- **Limit:** The survey announces sketches and omissions; copy its organization, not its proof-completeness standard for a new theorem.

### E03 — Bjorn Poonen, *Undecidability in Number Theory* (2008)

- **Text:** [primary text, university mirror](https://math.uchicago.edu/~shmuel/lg-readings/Poonen.pdf); [author's publications index](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/) independently identifies the article and prize. Published in *Notices AMS* 55, 344–350.
- **Reputation:** 2011 Chauvenet Prize, official MAA list and author index.
- **Inspection:** first two preprint pages: concrete equations, Hilbert's tenth problem, explanation of algorithms, initial definitions.
- **Craft to borrow:** Moves from answerable examples to a precise general decision problem and explains why a definition is needed. Clearly states what the article will cover. The prose carries conceptual meaning alongside formulas.
- **Limit:** Historical open-problem statements in this old survey must be rechecked before reuse; this survey was assessed for craft, not current number-theoretic status.

### E04 — Tom Leinster, *Rethinking Set Theory* (2014; preprint 2012)

- **Text:** [arXiv:1212.6543v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.6543v1), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1212.6543v1); [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.4169/amer.math.monthly.121.05.403).
- **Reputation:** 2019 Chauvenet Prize, official MAA list.
- **Inspection:** preprint pp. 1–2, “Why rethink?”, informal axiom summary, explanation of encoding objections.
- **Craft to borrow:** Begins with the reader's existing mathematical practice, identifies a tension, previews the replacement, and anticipates a natural objection. An informal summary is visibly distinguished from the actual axioms.
- **Limit:** Persuasive exposition about foundations; an informal axiom summary is not a substitute for formal definitions.

### E05 — Jordan S. Ellenberg, *Geometry, Inference, Complexity, and Democracy* (2021)

- **Text:** [arXiv:2006.10879v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10879v1), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.10879v1); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/bull/1708).
- **Reputation:** 2025 Chauvenet Prize, official MAA list; date and DOI explicitly listed there.
- **Inspection:** abstract and §1, preprint pp. 1–3.
- **Craft to borrow:** Establishes the real question, compares possible meanings of representation, and makes the difficulty of precision part of the explanation. The abstract states the interface among disciplines and the survey's intended contribution.
- **Limit:** Its contextual breadth serves a survey audience. A technical paper should scale the opening to its own problem and readers.

### E06 — Andrew Granville, *It Is Easy to Determine Whether a Given Integer Is Prime* (2005)

- **Text:** [published article, mathematician-hosted mirror](https://wstein.org/129-05/refs/Granville-It_is_easy_to_determine_whether_a_given_integer_is_prime.pdf); [author's exposition index](https://intra.dms.umontreal.ca/~andrew/expository.php).
- **Reputation:** 2008 Chauvenet Prize, official MAA list and author index.
- **Inspection:** published pp. 3–5, opening historical framing and computational motivation.
- **Craft to borrow:** Connects a familiar question to the modern need for a complexity measure. Context is used to explain what changed and why the result matters. The reader is told which conceptual distinction makes the breakthrough significant.

### E08 — John Milnor, *Classification of (n−1)-Connected 2n-Dimensional Manifolds and the Discovery of Exotic Spheres*

- **Text:** [primary text, university mirror](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/milnor.pdf).
- **Reputation:** Same author-level Steele evidence as E07; selected historical exposition, not an article-specific award claim.
- **Inspection:** first two PDF pages, including the initial choice of problem class and geometric reduction.
- **Craft to borrow:** Explains why a restricted class of objects is a productive starting point. The reader can see the research choice behind the technical statement; historical sequence is tied to a mathematical obstacle.
- **Limit:** Historical retrospective, not a template for every proof. Publication metadata/DOI not resolved during this survey.

### E09 — Vladimir Pozdnyakov and J. Michael Steele, *Buses, Bullies, and Bijections* (2016)

- **Text:** [author-hosted published PDF](https://vladimir-pozdnyakov.github.io/papers/BBB.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4169/math.mag.89.3.167).
- **Reputation:** 2020 Chauvenet Prize; official [MAA award announcement](https://maa.org/news/mathematical-association-of-america-awards-authors-the-chauvenet-and-david-p-robbins-prize-at-2020-joint-mathematics-meeting/) and recipients list.
- **Inspection:** published pp. 167–169: probability problem, small cases, representations of permutations.
- **Craft to borrow:** A single running example earns several representations. Notation is introduced when a concrete calculation becomes awkward. This is especially useful when explaining why a new encoding or invariant helps.
- **Limit:** Elementary expository level; the transferable feature is representation design, not its amount of elementary detail.

### What to imitate from Stacks, and what to add for a research paper

| Dimension | Stacks-style reference unit | Research paper or research-level lecture unit |
|---|---|---|
| Primary reading task | Retrieve an exact definition or reusable result | Understand a new question, result, and mechanism |
| Imitate | Explicit hypotheses and conventions; stable references; localized statements | The same precision, plus a problem-led route through the material |
| Context | Often supplied by nearby indexed reference material | Supply the particular reason the reader needs this object or reduction |
| Proof architecture | Fine-grained reusable units | Give an overview when it reduces reconstruction; extract lemmas for conceptual work |
| Generality | A broad reference can amortize technical setup across many applications | State intended generality honestly and explain its cost; postpone peripheral machinery |
| Avoid | Assuming that tag structure alone explains why the mathematics matters | Padding the text with roadmaps that merely repeat headings or unsupported importance claims |

The contrast concerns genre, not rigor. FAC and GAGA show that precise specialist research can have explanatory introductions; *Morse Theory* shows that a motivating model can lead directly to exact theorem statements. Stacks' local precision and these papers' global architecture can coexist in the same manuscript.

## Referee and editorial guidance

These are actual institutional policies or guides, except R08, which is a mathematician's proposed software-review practice. They supply useful criteria for an **internal simulated referee of the author's own draft**. They do not authorize uploading a confidential manuscript received as a real journal referee; policies differ and must be checked for that role.

### R01 — Mathematical Association of America, *Guide for Referees*

- **Primary source:** [MAA guide](https://maa.org/guide-for-referees/).
- **Inspection:** entire substantive guide: “AI Policy,” “Refereeing,” report-template outline, schedules.
- **Content:** Targets mathematicians who are novices in the article's topic; invites constructive advice; distinguishes author responsibility from referee assessment. Report structure separates summary, general assessment, major suggestions, and locally referenced minor changes. This is a particularly useful style-referee template.
- **Specific constraint:** Its real journal-referee policy prohibits putting unpublished manuscripts into AI tools and using third-party AI to generate the referee report. Author-side simulation is a different activity.

### R02 — MAA, *Referee's Report: Title of Paper* (Mathematics Magazine template)

- **Primary source:** [two-page PDF](https://maa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Referee-Report-Template.pdf).
- **Inspection:** both pages via extracted text.
- **Content:** Concrete report scaffold with constructive criticism, major suggestions, and page/paragraph/line minor comments. Encourages attention to the author's work and journal fit.
- **Inference for agents:** Demand a replacement or a diagnosis attached to an exact passage. A request to be “harsh” is less operational than this template.

### R03 — SIAM Journal on Mathematics of Data Science, *Instructions for Referees*

- **Primary source:** [SIAM instructions](https://epubs.siam.org/simods/instructions-for-referees).
- **Inspection:** Part I, “Duties,” “Content of the Report,” scientific/context/presentation/mathematics questions.
- **Content:** Evaluates novelty, correctness, and significance separately; asks for candor about expertise and completeness of review. It specifically asks about title, abstract, context, notation, organization, and figures, while keeping authors responsible for content.
- **Inference for agents:** Record the finite scope actually checked. Separate a clarity objection from a technical error and from a judgment of significance.

### R04 — European Mathematical Society, *Code of Practice for Mathematical Publication* (approved March 19, 2025)

- **Primary source:** [EMS code](https://euromathsoc.org/code-of-practice).
- **Inspection:** responsibilities of authors, editors, and referees; referee items 1–10.
- **Content:** Correctness, significance, novelty, clarity, constructive reporting, objectivity, timeliness, conflicts, confidentiality, and author responsibility. It addresses AI disclosure to editors and external-service uploads.
- **Inference for agents:** Keep factual findings separate from judgments, with respectful criticism and documented limits. Do not equate a generated referee-style report with certification or independent mathematical truth.

### R05 — London Mathematical Society, *Ethical Policy for Journals* (updated October 2026)

- **Primary source:** [LMS policy](https://www.lms.ac.uk/publications/policies/ethicalpolicy).
- **Inspection:** §3 “Reviewer Code,” especially §§3.2, 3.4–3.7; §4.6 editor AI restrictions also inspected.
- **Content:** Requires fair, supported criticism rather than personal criticism; separates confidential review obligations from authoring. Its reviewer AI policy restricts reviewing submitted manuscripts with AI; editing a referee's own already-written feedback is treated separately.
- **Inference for agents:** “Nit-picking PhD student” should mean finding specific reader obstacles, never inventing objections or adopting contempt. Use author-owned drafts for internal simulations.

### R06 — Illinois Journal of Mathematics, *Review Process*

- **Primary source:** [IJM process](https://ijm.math.illinois.edu/review-process/).
- **Inspection:** full substantive page.
- **Content:** Rates exposition, originality, interest, and overall quality separately. Editors should be reasonably certain of correctness; review time should reflect length and technical difficulty.
- **Inference for agents:** Keep separate fields for mathematical validity, exposition, and relevance. A high style score cannot offset a proof gap.

### R07 — London Mathematical Society, *Guide to Authors*

- **Primary source:** [11-page official PDF](https://www.lms.ac.uk/sites/lms.ac.uk/files/Publications/AuthorGuide.pdf).
- **Inspection:** “What happens next,” “Referee selection and referee's report,” printed pp. 8–9; opening process summary.
- **Content:** Explains quick opinions versus full refereeing, and asks about originality, correctness, importance, interest, achievement relative to length, comparison with related work, and exposition. Provides editorial process context rather than merely sentence-level style.
- **Inference for agents:** Run a short editorial architecture pass before expensive line editing. Assess whether the length is justified by the actual mathematical contribution.
- **Limit:** Older procedural guide; use the newer policy R05 for current AI restrictions.

### R08 — Jeroen Hanselman, *Guidelines for Writing and Reviewing Software in Computer Algebra* (2025)

- **Primary source:** [arXiv:2503.01541v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01541v1), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.01541v1).
- **Inspection:** §1 and opening review rationale; not every technical recommendation.
- **Content:** Extends mathematical reviewing to the computational artifacts on which some arguments rely, drawing attention to availability, reusability, and reproducibility.
- **Inference for agents:** A well-written computational claim needs a readable account of exactly what the code proves, accessible artifacts, and an appropriate audit. Prose polishing must not hide an unavailable computation.
- **Limit:** Practitioner proposal, not a journal-wide adopted policy and not a prose-quality experiment.

## Common lessons for writing-style.md and roles

These are our synthesis, with supporting source IDs; no single source is being cited as experimental validation.

1. **Retain rigor; stop optimizing for symbol density.** Kerodon's own opening motivates a mathematical question before definitions (E11). Ghrist and Leinster earn their machinery through a concrete deficiency (E02, E04). A useful policy is: “Use formulas for exact relationships; use prose to explain purpose, mechanism, and logical transitions.”
2. **Make reading cost the object of economy.** A brief overview or example may lengthen a page and shorten the reader's reconstruction time (E01, E07, E12). Deleting all context is not automatically concise.
3. **Specify the audience and genre.** Stacks-style reference material, a research introduction, and a survey require different proportions of motivation and reminders (E10–E14; R01). A paper for specialists should not rebuild an undergraduate course, but it must still explain the new move.
4. **Ask a first reader to reconstruct the architecture.** Require a 3–5 sentence account of the question, main result, and proof mechanism before detailed criticism. If it cannot be produced without guessing, diagnose the missing bridge (E07; R02–R03).
5. **Use separate bounded reports.** Mathematical soundness, sources, reader comprehension, and editorial significance are separate assessments (R03–R07). A style reviewer can flag an apparent mathematical ambiguity without silently repairing the argument and then certifying it.
6. **Demand actionable criticism with exact locations.** Each issue should name the reader's obstacle, its consequence, and one concrete repair, or state what information is missing (R01–R03, R05). No quota of criticisms and no reward for severity.
7. **Use exemplary passages as demonstrations, not author-name prompts.** Give an agent the relevant short passage plus the structural move to imitate: two-lemma architecture, example-to-definition transition, convention followed by caveat. “Write like Milnor” is too underspecified, and faithful imitation of surface mannerisms is not the objective.
